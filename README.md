# Business-Survival-Analysis
Analysis of 2020 business survival rates across eight U.S. industries, with a top-5 comparison and recommendation for a new business owner.
## The scenario

An entrepreneur wants to start a business and asks which industries give new businesses the best chance of surviving.

## Questions asked during the ask phase

1. **What does success look like for your business?**
   Success means the business is still operating after a set period of time.
2. **What time period should our analysis cover?**
   The past five years.
3. **How many top industries would you like us to highlight?**
   The top five, to give a broader set of options to work with.
4. **When do you need our findings?**
   In about one week.
5. **Am I presenting these findings directly to you?**
   Yes, the findings are presented directly to the entrepreneur.
U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data on business establishments, 2020 to 2025.
Steps
1. Collected the data for eight industries (Retail, Construction, Healthcare, Food Service, Education, Real Estate, Finance, and Transportation).
2. Cleaned each industry in its own sheet: checked for blanks, kept the original data untouched, and standardized the column names.
3. Built a summary table with 2025 surviving establishments, rate since birth, and average employees per survivor, using VLOOKUP to pull each industry's 2025 figures.
4. Sorted the industries by survival rate and built two column charts: all eight, and the top five.
Findings
• Retail (59.8%) and Food Service (59.3%) had the highest five-year survival rates.
• Healthcare had the most surviving establishments (83,101) but the lowest survival rate (52.6%).
• All eight industries fell between 52.6% and 59.8%, so no industry was dramatically safer than the others.
Recommendation
Start with Retail or Food Service, but don't choose on survival rate alone. Also weigh startup cost, the team's skills, and local demand, and plan for enough cash to last past the first few years.
Limits
• Covers only businesses started in 2020.
• Measures survival, not profit.
• Doesn't explain why businesses close.
Tools
Google Sheets (cleaning, VLOOKUP, charts), Canva (presentation).
